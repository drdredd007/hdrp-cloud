# Planetary HDRP water

The customized HDRP water pipeline supports camera-owned spherical oceans through `WaterGeometryType.Planetary` and `PlanetaryWaterRendering`. `PlanetHdrpWaterRenderer` supplies the state from an immutable EarthLike surface recipe and an authored `WaterSurface` prefab; no planet-sized float Transform is required.

Native FFT displacement, water Shader Graph, foam, GBuffer and deferred lighting remain in use. Ray/sphere intersections use double precision, while a separate R32 metric endpoint survives projection scaling for lighting, atmospheric composition and underwater absorption. Surface normals, refraction, environment reflection and light scattering use the radial up direction. Stock planar water keeps its original branch.

Each FFT band and the foam texture blend the same three planet-local projections, weighted by the radial normal to exclude edge-on charts. Wave surface gradients are projected onto the sphere tangent plane and rotated into world space before the native Shader Graph resolves the normal. This removes the latitude-dependent stripes caused by assigning different projections to different bands and passing tangent coordinates as world gradients.

Wrapped wave coordinates are used only for addressing. All four bicubic additional-data taps use explicit derivatives of the continuous physical position, so crossing a repeat boundary cannot select a distant mip and create a seam in normals or foam. Mip filtering remains enabled, including distant water.

Refraction reads camera-owned planet opaque endpoints as well as native camera depth. Foundation root endpoints are refined to the canonical seabed before the water pass, so coarse terrain chords cannot hide a shallow bottom through excessive absorption. The foreground/native depth still wins when closer. `Absorption Distance` is a visibility distance in metres, not an opacity slider: a positive value is required. The inspector minimum and runtime denominator are 0.01 m; existing authored zero values are preserved and behave as effectively opaque water.

The screen carrier uses direct clip-space vertices inside the depth range. The GBuffer writes physical depth before SSR/pre-refraction; writing the compressed carrier depth made SSR see 0.1 m instead of 30 m. The exact metric endpoint remains authoritative outside native projection precision, and is merged before atmospheric/cloud composition. Canonical seabed intersections prevent seeing the opposite ocean through the solid body from underwater. Endpoint validity is reset for each observer render.

For coordinate cameras with planetary water, independent AIR transport and native weather compose after water lighting. AIR is integrated once to the final endpoint, using the configured resolution divisor. The layer depth carries the exact water distance in R with G=1; alpha retains the nearest celestial endpoint. Native clouds consume this distance instead of a clamped hardware depth. Their AIR prefix also spans celestial distances beyond the ordinary camera far plane.

Celestial reflections enter the native sky fallback after local SSR and reflection probes, retaining their hierarchy weights and native Fresnel. Up to 16 camera-owned spherical bodies are considered. Visible surface points use the rendered planet colour; offscreen points use a cached 512×256 global-albedo atlas and unattenuated celestial light records. There is no arbitrary night-side light floor. Maps are resized in a 2D staging target, copied to explicit array slices and mipmapped only when the source changes. Offscreen local geology, relief silhouettes and secondary atmospheric scattering are approximations, not a separate reflected-camera render.

Both visible and offscreen celestial hits receive AIR transport along the reflected water-to-body segment. The integration clips to each atmosphere and combines overlapping extinction and source terms, using the same per-body density, phase and sunlight transmission as the planetary media renderer. Camera-to-water AIR is still applied once by the final compositor. Native volumetric clouds are traced along the reflected segment with the current camera's coverage, density, weather regions, wind and lighting; their transmission occludes the celestial surface and their radiance replaces the blocked contribution. The surrounding sky IBL uses the same cloud transport, so an opaque cloud erases the planet's reflected silhouette rather than leaving a differently shaded disk. AIR already present in sky IBL is not integrated twice. AIR in front of and behind clouds is split at the native opacity-weighted mean cloud distance. This split approximates a distributed cloud layer; it is not a coupled multiple-scattering solver. Ordinary local SSR and reflection probes remain first in their existing hierarchy.

14 October night-hemisphere correction: a compatible camera-local PBS atmosphere uses HDRP's single/multiple-scattering LUTs for both the reflected celestial surface and the adjacent visual sky. Compatibility is explicit in each camera-owned reflection record, independently of the stock PBR fog gate (disabled by independent view media). The selected sky's space-emission cubemap, inverse rotation and intensity supply the background before reflected AIR/cloud transport. A dark opaque body still occults that background; its unlit surface can converge to atmospheric sky radiance when the space background is dark. Other atmospheres retain the independent density/source integration; intervals and the native mean cloud distance preserve ordering. Overlapping native/independent atmosphere coupling and distributed cloud multiple scattering remain approximations. The usual selected-atmosphere interval skips the independent32-step march. This is not a60 FPS measurement.

The qualified target is PC/Direct3D 11, one view per camera. Canonical height coverage applies to global Foundation roots; regional/detail/stamp terrain uses native opaque occlusion. Global fluid simulation, spherical CPU buoyancy queries, globalized planar masks/decals and XR remain separate work.

Setup, screenshots, executable controls and failure history are documented in [SpaceRunner planetary water](https://github.com/drdredd007/SpaceRunner/blob/master/Docs/Coordinates/PLANET_HDRP_WATER_2026-10-13.md).
